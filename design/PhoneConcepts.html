Three ways to lay out the phone screen

Each phone below works: tap the dock, the cards or the capsule. The small screens under each phone are its key states. Tap one to load that state into the phone. Content is illustrative: three machines, six agents, two Claude accounts and one Codex account.

Concept A

Thumb Dock

Bottom-anchored, everything within thumb reach
9:41
Key states (tap to load)

The idea. Everything you navigate with lives in one dock at the bottom, where your thumb already is. The top of the screen is just terminal. Tap the agent capsule to raise a sheet. The usage and stats glance is at the top of the sheet, then machines, then spaces with their agents. The actions row sits nearest the thumb. The bell jumps to the agent that needs you and opens quick answers right there.

Better than today

  • Four stacked bars (top bar, strip, tabs, composer) become one dock. The reply row appears only when an agent waits or you open the keyboard.
  • Machine → space → agent is one sheet, all reachable one-handed. Nothing sits at the top edge.
  • Usage and stats get labelled glance cards instead of competing for top-bar width.

Trade-off: switching agents takes two taps (open sheet, pick), and there's no always-visible tab row to scan.

Concept B

Inbox

Agent-first: a queue of what needs you, terminal as detail
9:41
Key states (tap to load)

The idea. The home screen is a queue of agents that need you: waiting first, then finished. Each card shows the last terminal lines and the answer buttons, so most prompts get answered without opening the terminal. Step through the queue with the arrows (a swipe on the device). Opening a card turns the terminal into a detail view with the composer ready. Files and Changes open in a sheet there.

Better than today

  • Fastest path to a reply: one tap from launch, with the question and options visible on the card.
  • Usage rings and per-machine cpu / ram / disk become a dashboard header that doubles as the machine filter.
  • The attention strip and tab pills are gone. The queue and the All agents list replace both, grouped by machine and space.

Trade-off: watching a busy agent takes a step into detail. It's closer to a supervisor app than a terminal app.

Concept C

Capsule

Minimal chrome: one capsule that expands, glance pulls down
9:41
Key states (tap to load)

The idea. The terminal owns the screen. One floating capsule at the bottom names the machine and agent in front. Tap it and it expands into a three-step picker (machine › space › agent) with breadcrumbs, and a "Needs you" shortcut on the first step. Pull the top handle for a glance at usage and machine stats. When another agent waits, the capsule grows a rose count. When the agent in front waits, answer chips appear above the capsule.

Better than today

  • Most terminal height of the three. Chrome stays one capsule however many machines or accounts you add.
  • The hierarchy is explicit, one level at a time, with big rows, counts and waiting tags at each level.
  • The glance has room for large rings with real numbers per account and per machine.

Trade-off: everything is one layer deeper. A pull gesture on a device must not fight terminal scroll, so it starts from the handle only.

Kept from Nightglass: the wallpaper, 50% glass boxes, an 82% terminal pane, Inter for UI, JetBrains Mono for the terminal, ids and numbers, and the status colours (working green, waiting rose, warn amber from 60%). Touch targets are 40 px and up.

Changed for this board: macmini uses violet instead of rose, so a machine tint never reads as "waiting". Search, the action buttons and Files/Changes are stand-ins that show a toast or a sample list. Swipes and the pull-down are buttons here.
